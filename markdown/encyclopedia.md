# World Encyclopedia of Eryndor

*Version 0.2 (Working Canon)*

---

# Introduction

Eryndor is a world shaped by cycles of destruction, rebirth, migration, and reconciliation. Ancient celestial wars shattered worlds, transformed peoples, and left scars that still shape the modern age. Though much of that history has faded into myth, its consequences remain visible across every continent.

Today the known world consists of six major regions:

* Eldressa
* Velkrand
* Ilyndor
* The Meridian Isles (The Freewaters)
* Skeldran
* Hrothan

The modern era is one of recovery, exploration, trade, diplomacy, and cautious cooperation between peoples who once viewed one another as enemies.

# Cosmology

**Status:** Working Canon

Before the rise of mortal civilization, existence was shaped by three immortal powers:

* Angels
* Devils
* Dragons

The Dragons were tasked with maintaining balance between the opposing cosmic principles embodied by the Angels and Devils.

Within the Mortal Plane, the Angels came to embody:

* Creation
* Preservation
* Continuity
* Aspiration
* Flourishing life

The Devils came to embody:

* Transformation
* Entropy
* Change
* Destruction
* Renewal through endings

Neither force was considered inherently good or evil.

Both were understood to be necessary for existence itself.

The Dragons alone were meant to understand this balance fully.

For untold ages the balance held.

Eventually the Dragons grew increasingly detached from their cosmic purpose. Many became fascinated with the material worlds, mortal civilizations, beauty, wealth, and individual ambition.

As Dragonkind drifted from its role as cosmic arbiter, the balance between Angels and Devils began to fracture.

This failure would culminate in the Celestial War.

# Greater Cosmology

**Status:** Working Canon

Existence is divided into several great cosmological layers.

Though mortals often interpret these realms through religion and mythology, most scholars agree that the structure of reality predates recorded history.

---

## The Hallows Above

The Hallows Above are the upper divine realms occupied by the New Gods and allied divine powers.

These realms are associated with:

* Light
* Civilization
* Knowledge
* Creation
* Protection
* Order
* The celestial heavens

The gods of the Hallows Above possess immense influence over the Mortal Plane but are unable to freely manifest within it without great sacrifice or consequence.

Many religions across Eryndor worship deities believed to reside within the Hallows Above.

Clerics and Paladins commonly derive their divine power from these gods.

---

## The Circles Below

The Circles Below are the lower divine realms occupied by the Fallen Gods and other exiled divine powers.

These realms are associated with:

* Corruption
* Ruin
* Violence
* Forbidden knowledge
* Divine punishment
* Destructive ambition

Despite common misconception, the Circles Below are not connected to the Devils or Demons of the Celestial War.

The Devils originated within the Mortal Plane itself.

The Circles Below instead contain ancient divine beings banished during the Divine War that predates the creation of the Mortal Plane.

Warlocks, cultists, and forbidden sects often draw power from entities believed to dwell within these realms.

---

## The Mortal Plane

The Mortal Plane is the physical cosmological layer in which all known mortal history takes place.

It was created following the ancient Divine War as part of a cosmic balance brokered between the gods of the Hallows Above and the Circles Below.

Within the Mortal Plane existed three great prime worlds:

* Eryndor
* Gontkhael
* Nythorak

The Angels, Devils, and Dragons were created or placed within these worlds as balancing cosmic forces intended to preserve equilibrium within the Mortal Plane itself.

The Gods themselves were forbidden from directly interfering with this balance, though many continued to influence events subtly through visions, omens, divine magic, and chosen followers.

The Celestial War marked the collapse of this original balance.

---

## The Realms Beyond

Beyond the core cosmological structure exist countless lesser realms and fractured realities collectively known as the Realms Beyond.

These include:

* The Elemental Lands
* The Astral Plane
* Spirit Realms
* Dream Realms
* Fragment Worlds
* Ancient shattered realities
* The Void

Many scholars believe numerous lesser realms were created or destabilized during the Shattering.

Accessing these realms typically requires powerful magic, ancient gateways, divine intervention, or specialized knowledge.

---

## The Void

The Void predates the Shattering and exists beyond the boundaries of the Mortal Plane.

It is not considered a realm of evil, punishment, or death.

Rather, the Void is understood as:

* endless cosmic darkness,
* the absence surrounding reality,
* and the infinite expanse between worlds.

The Shattering did not create the Void.

However, the destruction of Gontkhael and the banishment of Nythorak greatly weakened the boundaries separating the Void from the Mortal Plane.

Many scholars believe prolonged exposure to the Void warps reality, memory, time, and living beings themselves.

---

# The Three Prime Worlds

**Status:** Working Canon

Before the Shattering, the Mortal Plane was centered around three great worlds bound together in cosmic balance.

---

## Eryndor

Eryndor was the mortal world.

Unlike Gontkhael and Nythorak, Eryndor survived the Shattering largely intact.

Most modern civilizations trace their origins to Eryndor following the collapse of the Celestial Age.

Though spared outright destruction, Eryndor was permanently altered by the Shattering and its aftermath.

As Gontkhael was destroyed and Nythorak banished into the Void, the ancient cycle of souls collapsed.

Displaced souls from the dying worlds began reincarnating upon Eryndor outside the old cycle for the first time.

Over countless generations these souls gradually became the mortal races known today.

Many scholars believe traces of their original worlds still linger within them:

Elves retaining the strongest connection to the Angelic legacy of Gontkhael,
Deonati carrying spiritual ties to the primal forces of nature and the Beastlands,
and humanity adapting most fully to life outside the ancient cycle itself.

Death upon Eryndor became more final than it had been within the old cosmic balance, with mortal souls now believed to pass onward into the Astral Plane after death.

Many scholars believe the instability created during the Shattering fractured portions of reality itself, giving rise to many of the lesser realms and planar anomalies known today.

---

## Gontkhael

Gontkhael was the world of the Angels.

It was associated with:

* Creation
* Preservation
* Flourishing life
* Magical abundance
* Continuity

Ancient myths describe Gontkhael as breathtakingly beautiful and saturated with primordial magic.

At the center of Gontkhael rested the Soulstone — the metaphysical heart of the world itself.

During the Final Invocation, the Soulstone was harnessed in an attempt to end the Celestial War.

The Soulstone shattered.

Gontkhael physically broke apart.

The world collapsed into drifting fragments, ruined celestial debris, and shattered continents suspended within dead space.

Though technically still accessible, Gontkhael is considered a dead world.

Modern Demon traditions often refer to it as:

### The Broken Heaven

---

## Nythorak

Nythorak was the world of the Devils.

Unlike many later mythologies portray, Nythorak was not originally a realm of torment or hellfire.

It was a vibrant and living world associated with:

* Transformation
* Entropy
* Renewal
* Endings
* Motion
* Change

Ancient descriptions portray Nythorak as a world in constant evolution:

* shifting continents,
* migrating forests,
* volcanic renewal,
* violent storms,
* and endlessly changing ecosystems.

Life on Nythorak was abundant, but rarely static.

Following the Final Invocation, Nythorak was banished into the Void alongside the surviving Devils.

Cut off from the cosmic cycle and isolated for untold ages, the world slowly withered.

Modern accounts describe Nythorak as:

* cold,
* hollow,
* dim,
* and dying.

Many Devils now view themselves not as conquerors, but as exiles from a fading world trapped beyond reality.

## The Cycle of Souls

Before the Shattering, the three prime worlds existed within a delicate metaphysical balance.

Souls did not remain bound permanently to a single world.

When mortal beings died upon Gontkhael, their souls eventually passed into Nythorak.

When beings died upon Nythorak, their souls eventually passed into Gontkhael.

This exchange formed an eternal cycle of transformation between the worlds.

The Angels and Devils both served essential roles within this process.

The Angels nurtured flourishing life, continuity, and creation.

The Devils oversaw transformation, endings, renewal, and the passage beyond death.

Neither force was considered inherently righteous or evil.

Both were understood to be necessary for existence itself.

The Dragons were tasked with maintaining equilibrium between these opposing cosmic principles.

The Rite of Continuance created by Orelas disrupted this balance.

By extending mortal life far beyond its natural limits, the cycle of souls between worlds began to fail.

This disruption ultimately triggered the chain of events that became the Celestial War.

---

# The Divine War

**Status:** Working Canon

Long before the Celestial War, the gods themselves fought a catastrophic conflict known as the Divine War.

The victors became known as the New Gods and claimed the Hallows Above.

The defeated gods became known as the Fallen Gods and were banished into the Circles Below.

Following the war, the opposing divine powers helped create the Mortal Plane as part of a cosmic balance intended to prevent further divine catastrophe.

The Angels, Devils, and Dragons were all tied to this balance in different ways.

Though the gods were forbidden from directly ruling the Mortal Plane, many scholars believe subtle divine influence continued long after its creation.

Some modern traditions even speculate that certain divine powers may have quietly encouraged the growing imbalance that eventually led to the Celestial War itself.

# Historical Eras

**Status:** Working Canon

## The Celestial Age

The age of immortal powers.

Dragons maintained balance between Angels and Devils.

Mortal races did not yet exist.

---

## The Celestial War

The Celestial War began not through conquest or simple malice, but through the disruption of the cosmic balance between worlds.

### The Rite of Continuance

In the final ages before the war, the Angelic mage Orelas devoted himself to the care of an early mortal people believed to be precursors to modern Elves.

After witnessing countless mortal lives end in suffering and extinction, Orelas dedicated himself to discovering a means of preserving mortal life.

After centuries of study, Orelas created the Rite of Continuance — a form of soul-binding magic that granted dramatically extended lifespans.

This act unknowingly disrupted the cycle between Gontkhael and Nythorak.

Souls no longer passed properly between worlds.

The Devils recognized the imbalance almost immediately and appealed to the Dragons for intervention.

The Dragons did not act.

---

### The First Massacre

As the imbalance worsened and negotiations failed, the Devil General Maelthoryx led an assault against the immortalized proto-elven civilization protected by Orelas.

The resulting slaughter became the first truly irreversible atrocity of the era.

The Angelic warrior Valacir managed to hold back the Devil host long enough for surviving refugees to escape, slaying Maelthoryx before falling himself.

Most historians and mythic traditions consider this massacre to be the true beginning of the Celestial War.

---

### Escalation

What began as an attempt to restore balance rapidly escalated into total war.

The Angels viewed the slaughter as unforgivable brutality.

The Devils viewed the imbalance as an existential threat to reality itself.

As the conflict expanded across worlds, the Dragons repeatedly failed to intervene decisively.

This period became known in later history as:

### The Silence of Dragons

A phrase still used in the modern era to symbolize catastrophic inaction in the face of growing disaster.

---

### The Burning of Gontkhael

As the war continued, Gontkhael itself began collapsing beneath the strain of the conflict.

Ancient accounts describe:

* Entire cities vanishing
* Oceans boiling
* Reality fracturing
* Magic destabilizing
* Regions consumed by celestial fire

By this stage, many among both Angels and Devils had come to believe the war could no longer be stopped.

---

### The Soulstone

Deep beneath Gontkhael rested a vast primordial crystal known as the Soulstone.

The Soulstone was believed to contain the metaphysical essence of the world itself.

The Angelic scholar Zalethira first uncovered references to the Soulstone while studying the deep foundations of Gontkhael.

Although fascinated by its existence, she repeatedly warned that no living being should ever attempt to wield its power directly.

After Zalethira's death during the later years of the war, her apprentice Amareth continued her research in secret.

As Gontkhael neared destruction, Amareth ultimately discovered how the Soulstone could be harnessed.

---

### The Final Invocation

Facing annihilation, the Angels used the Soulstone as a final desperate act to end the war.

The resulting event became known as the Final Invocation.

The Devils were defeated and cast into the Void.

But the Soulstone shattered.

Gontkhael broke apart.

Reality itself fractured.

The surviving Angels were permanently transformed.

This event became known as:

### The Shattering

---

## The Shattering

**Status:** Working Canon

The Shattering marked the end of the Celestial Age and permanently altered existence itself.

During the Final Invocation, the Angels harnessed the Soulstone at the heart of Gontkhael in a desperate attempt to end the Celestial War.

The Soulstone shattered.

Gontkhael physically broke apart.

Entire continents fractured and drifted into dead space as the metaphysical structure of the world collapsed.

At the same time, Nythorak and the surviving Devils were banished into the Void beyond the Mortal Plane.

Eryndor survived largely intact, though reality itself was permanently scarred by the catastrophe.

Ancient pathways between worlds collapsed.

Planar boundaries weakened.

Fragments of reality splintered away into lesser realms and isolated dimensions.

Many scholars believe the Shattering created or destabilized many of the Realms Beyond known today.

The surviving Angels emerged transformed into the beings now known as Demons.

Their once radiant forms became scarred by lingering Soulstone energy.

Their immortality faded.

Their wings darkened.

Their souls remained permanently fractured.

Many modern myths refer to the aftermath of the Shattering as:

### The Ashen Dawn

The moment the surviving Angels realized they had ended the war at the cost of their own world and identity.

---

## Age of Rebirth

Life slowly returned to the surviving worlds.

Nature reclaimed much that had been lost.

The Beastlands sent divine emissaries into the recovering world.

# Geography of the Known World

The known world is centered around six major regions.

---

## Eldressa

**Status:** Canon

Eldressa is the historical and cultural heart of civilization.

Most recorded history originates there.

Many of the world's oldest kingdoms, institutions, and traditions began on Eldressa.

Major regions include:

* Valtiron
* Rivara
* Woods of Brethil
* Lanaryan Desert
* Claran Mountains
* Danac Hills
* Florian Plains
* Coral
* Yfrathas

Eldressa is fertile, populous, and politically complex.

It remains the most influential continent in the world.

### Broad Historical Identity

Eldressa was the first great cradle of mortal civilization following the Shattering.

The earliest major Elven kingdoms arose within its forests, river valleys, and fertile plains beneath the guidance of the Dragon Stewards.

The Claran Mountains later became home to the first great Dwarven holds, whose expansion eventually brought them into conflict with the growing Elven dominions.

Many of the oldest surviving wars, treaties, ruins, and myths of the mortal world originate from Eldressa.

Over time:

* Halfling trade cultures flourished across the Danac Hills and Florian Plains,
* Human migrations arrived from Hrothan,
* and the continent gradually evolved into the political and cultural center of the known world.

Though countless kingdoms have risen and fallen throughout its history, Eldressa remains the continent most deeply shaped by the legacy of the First Mortal Age.

### Modern Political Structure

Eldressa is not a unified nation.

Power is fragmented among numerous states, city-states, kingdoms, and regional governments.

Valtiron serves as the political meeting place of the continent.

The council convening there functions similarly to an international assembly rather than a central government.

### Rivara

Rivara is the commercial capital of Eldressa.

Its power derives from:

* Trade
* Finance
* Diplomacy
* Transportation

Rather than direct military authority.

---

## Velkrand

**Status:** Canon

Velkrand is the industrial powerhouse of the known world.

The continent is defined by:

* Rail networks
* Artificers
* Mining operations
* Foundries
* Skyports
* Engineering guilds

Dominant peoples include:

* Goblins
* Gnomes
* Dwarves
* Humans
* Warforged
* Mechagnomes

### Broad Historical Identity

Velkrand developed largely separate from the ancient political struggles of Eldressa.

The continent became the homeland of:

* Goblins,
* Gnomes,
* Firbolgs,
* and eventually the Clank.

Ancient traditions suggest Goblins and Gnomes may share distant common ancestry dating back to the earliest rebirths following the Shattering.

Over time, however, the two peoples developed radically different cultures and philosophies.

The Goblins of northern and western Velkrand emerged from volcanic mountains, deep caverns, and harsh industrial regions, developing societies centered around mechanical ingenuity, survival, and resource extraction.

The Gnomes of southern and eastern Velkrand developed within lush forests, cliffside settlements, and extensive tunnel networks, becoming associated with magical engineering, arcane craftsmanship, and refined construction.

For much of their history the Goblins and Gnomes warred constantly over Velkrand's immense natural resources.

The destruction of the Great Mountain during the First Demon War devastated both civilizations through tsunamis and volcanic catastrophe.

The resulting reconstruction efforts led to an unprecedented era of cooperation between Goblins and Gnomes, eventually giving rise to the industrial foundations of modern Velkrand and the creation of the Clank.


### Modern Political Structure

Velkrand remains politically fragmented.

However, a growing movement toward continental consolidation exists.

Mining consortiums, industrial unions, trade coalitions, and artificer guilds increasingly wield power traditionally held by governments.

Many believe Velkrand will eventually become the first truly unified continent in the modern age.

---

## Ilyndor

**Status:** Canon

Ilyndor is the great wilderness continent.

It is defined by:

* Ancient forests
* Spirit-touched lands
* Mountain valleys
* Primal ecosystems
* Beastlands nexuses

Ilyndor serves as the modern cultural homeland of the Deonati.

### Broad Historical Identity

Ilyndor developed very differently from the great kingdoms and empires of Eldressa.

Rather than centralized nations, the peoples of Ilyndor formed ancient regional cultures, spiritual traditions, tribal confederations, and sacred territories deeply tied to the natural world.

This decentralization did not make Ilyndor primitive.

Many Ilyndoran cultures developed immense sophistication in:

* oral history,
* artistry,
* ritual tradition,
* ecological stewardship,
* natural magic,
* and spiritual philosophy.

The continent was historically shaped by the Woodland Guardians — ancient primordial beings who presided over the Heartwood and the deepest forests of Ilyndor.

Though often worshipped as gods by outsiders, the Woodland Guardians are mortal beings of immense age and spiritual power rather than true deities.

The Deonati are believed to descend spiritually from these ancient guardians and the primal forces they embodied.

While some Deonati later migrated to Eldressa long before the First Demon War, Ilyndor remains their oldest and most sacred homeland.

### Government

Ilyndor possesses no unified government.

Most settlements are:

* Clan-based
* Local
* Spiritual
* Independent

Authority comes largely from tradition and local leadership.

### The Heartwood

The Heartwood is the spiritual center of Ilyndor.

The ancient beasts dwelling there possess immense cultural influence.

Though they rule no kingdom, their wishes are widely respected.

Their authority is moral and spiritual rather than political.

---

## The Meridian Isles (The Freewaters)

**Status:** Canon

The Meridian Isles sit at the center of the known world.

They form the crossroads of global trade.

No continent exerts lasting control over them.

### Meridia

Meridia is the most important city in the world.

It serves as:

* Trade hub
* Diplomatic hub
* Cultural crossroads

When world leaders meet, they meet in Meridia.

Its neutrality is one of its greatest strengths.

### Nadris

Nadris serves as Meridia's shadow.

Smugglers, privateers, black markets, and illicit commerce flourish there.

Though officially condemned, Nadris and Meridia remain economically inseparable.

---

## Skeldran

**Status:** Canon

Skeldran is a shattered northern continent and the oldest known ruin of the First Age.

Long before the rise of the great mortal kingdoms, Skeldran was home to the Leviathans—immense primordial beings that served as the continent's First Stewards. Alongside them flourished several ancient peoples, including the Selkari, the Tidemarked, and the Corvani.

For reasons now lost to history, the Leviathans eventually turned upon one another in a conflict remembered only as the Leviathan War.

The war lasted generations and reshaped the continent itself.

Mountains collapsed.

Coastlines shattered.

Entire regions sank beneath the sea.

When the conflict finally ended, the Leviathans had been driven to extinction and Skeldran was left broken beyond recovery.

The descendants of its ancient peoples either scattered across the world or vanished entirely.

Today Skeldran consists of:

* Fractured landmasses
* Drowned valleys
* Black fjords
* Obsidian cliffs
* Dangerous channels
* Storm-wracked seas
* Fossilized Leviathan remains

Massive skeletons protrude from cliffsides, beaches, islands, and mountain ranges throughout the continent. In some regions entire settlements have been built within the ribs and vertebrae of long-dead Leviathans.

The continent feels ancient, hostile, and haunted by a catastrophe so old that even its causes have been forgotten.

### Population

Permanent settlements are rare.

Greywatch remains one of the only significant inhabited locations.

Most of Skeldran consists of ruins, abandoned settlements, forgotten strongholds, ancient burial sites, and dangerous coastlines.

The few inhabitants who remain are typically explorers, treasure hunters, fishermen, scholars, scavengers, or those seeking isolation from the rest of the world.

Stories persist of hidden Selkari enclaves, isolated Corvani descendants, and forgotten communities living amongst the bones of the Leviathans, though few such tales have ever been verified.

### The Dragon Waters

The seas surrounding Skeldran are among the most dangerous in the world.

Dense fog, obsidian reefs, sea stacks, violent currents, and ancient predators make navigation perilous.

Many sailors believe the seas never fully recovered from the Leviathan War.

Storms can appear without warning and entire vessels occasionally vanish without explanation.

The waters are littered with hidden shoals, submerged ruins, and the skeletal remains of Leviathans whose bones still lie beneath the waves.

Few captains willingly sail these routes unless necessity leaves them no alternative.

---

## Hrothan

**Status:** Canon

Hrothan is the frozen southern homeland of humanity.

It is vast, bleak, ancient, and unforgiving.

The continent is defined by:

* Tundra
* Glaciers
* Frozen rivers
* Ice shelves
* Mountain ranges
* Frozen inland seas

### Broad Historical Identity

Hrothan changed little across the ages.

Unlike the southern continents, it never developed vast centralized kingdoms or continent-spanning empires.

Instead, survival itself became the defining force shaping the peoples of the north.

The continent remained dominated by:

* roaming human tribes,
* Giantkin,
* Goliath clans,
* ancient beasts,
* and brutal seasonal migrations.

Time, cold, famine, and distance became far greater enemies than politics or conquest.

The oral traditions of Hrothan preserve some of the oldest surviving human myths upon Eryndor, including stories of the First Crossing and ancient Dragon-guided migrations southward into Eldressa.

Many southern historians view Hrothan as primitive.

The peoples of Hrothan generally view the south as soft, fleeting, and disconnected from the old truths of the world.

### Human Origins

Humanity traces its origins to Hrothan.

Long before recorded history, human tribes migrated northward from the frozen continent.

Ancient migration routes still cross the continent.

These routes are marked by:

* Cairns
* Standing stones
* Burial mounds
* Primitive shrines
* Ancient campsites

### Modern Population

Permanent settlements are extremely rare.

Most inhabitants are:

* Nomadic humans
* Mammoth hunters
* Goliath clans
* Giantkin tribes

One major geothermal refuge exists in the eastern mountains.

Even there, civilization resembles a survival camp rather than a true city.

# Peoples of Eryndor

## Humans

Originating from Hrothan.

Now the most widespread people in the world.

Adaptable, diverse, and highly decentralized.

---

## Elves

One of the oldest mortal civilizations.

Strong ties to Eldressa.

Many ancient myths trace Elven origins back to the immortalized proto-elven peoples protected by Orelas before the Celestial War.

Major groups include:

* High Elves
* Wood Elves
* Sea Elves
* Drow
* Primal Elves

---

## Dwarves

Originating in the Claran Mountains.

Renowned for engineering, craftsmanship, and fortifications.

Present throughout Eldressa and Velkrand.

---

## Halflings

Centered around:

* Danac Hills
* Florian Plains

Known for:

* Trade
* Agriculture
* Caravans
* Hospitality

---

## Deonati

Shapeshifters descended from ancient animal spirits.

Originated through the influence of divine beings from the Beastlands.

Once concentrated in Eldressa.

Now primarily associated with Ilyndor.

Modern Deonati society continues to rebuild after centuries of persecution and displacement.

---

## Demons

Descendants of the Angels of Gontkhael.

The surviving Angels were transformed during the Shattering after the destruction of the Soulstone.

Their once radiant forms became scarred and altered by lingering Soulstone energy.

Modern Demons preserve fragments of ancient Angelic culture, language, and philosophy, though much has been lost over millennia.

Many Demons view themselves as descendants of a people who won the Celestial War only to lose everything in the process.

Many Demon traditions preserve myths and mourning rituals tied to Nythorak and the destruction of Gontkhael.

Among many Demons, the Celestial War is remembered less as a victory and more as the death of two worlds and the collapse of the balance that once sustained existence itself.

---

## Dragons

Ancient guardians of cosmic balance.

The Dragons were once responsible for maintaining equilibrium between the Angels and Devils.

During the years leading into the Celestial War, many Dragons became increasingly detached from their cosmic purpose and more fascinated by the material worlds and mortal civilizations.

Their repeated failure to intervene decisively during the growing conflict became known as:

### The Silence of Dragons

Modern Dragon culture still carries immense inherited shame tied to this era.

Following the Shattering, many Dragons took it upon themselves to guide and protect the newly emerging mortal races upon Eryndor.

Burdened by guilt over their failure during the Celestial War, they became stewards, teachers, mediators, and guardians during the earliest ages of mortal civilization.

Not all Dragons embraced this responsibility.

Some withdrew into isolation.

Others became consumed by greed, territorial ambition, or obsession with preserving what remained of the old world.

Nevertheless, much of early mortal civilization developed beneath the distant guidance and protection of Dragonkind.

Though once believed lost, Dragons have now returned to the modern world.

# Mythic Figures of the Celestial War

**Status:** Working Canon

The following figures are among the most important surviving mythological personalities associated with the Celestial War.

Due to the immense age of these events, many details remain contradictory or uncertain.

---

## Aurelioneth — The First Voice of Heaven

Aurelioneth was the foremost political and philosophical leader of the Angels during the final centuries before the war.

He believed existence could transcend suffering and limitation through creation and preservation.

Modern traditions remember him as compassionate, brilliant, and tragically idealistic.

---

## Orelas — The Shepherd of the Firstborn

A wandering Angelic mage and caretaker who created the Rite of Continuance.

Orelas is believed to have been responsible for the extended lifespans of the proto-elven peoples.

His death during the First Massacre is widely considered the moment diplomacy became impossible.

---

## Seraphelle — The Golden Spear

One of the greatest Angelic military commanders of the war.

Seraphelle led many of the largest Angelic offensives after the outbreak of full-scale conflict.

---

## Zalethira — The Veiled Star

A legendary Angelic scholar who first uncovered the existence of the Soulstone.

She warned repeatedly against attempts to wield its power directly.

---

## Amareth — The Last Disciple

The apprentice of Zalethira.

Amareth ultimately discovered how the Soulstone could be harnessed during the final years of the war.

His ultimate fate remains unknown.

---

## Valacir — The Ashen Martyr

An Angelic warrior who died defending survivors during the massacre led by Maelthoryx.

He became a lasting symbol of sacrifice and duty among later Demon traditions.

---

## Vaelzira — The Keeper of the Turning Wheel

The foremost philosophical leader of the Devils during the final age before the war.

Vaelzira argued that existence depended upon balance, transformation, and renewal.

She ultimately sanctioned military action after the Dragons refused to intervene.

---

## Tharozhul — The Iron Maw

A feared Devil General who advocated increasingly ruthless warfare as the conflict escalated.

Some legends claim he survived the Final Invocation and still wanders beyond reality.

---

## Maelthoryx — The Pale Huntress

The Devil General who led the massacre of Orelas and the proto-elves.

She was slain by Valacir during the battle.

---

## Nyssara — The Ember Blade

A Devil warrior remembered for protecting civilians and refugees during the later stages of the war.

Many surviving Devil epics portray her as one of the few figures who sought to end the conflict before the Final Invocation.

---

## Aurymathrax — The Worldscale

The foremost Dragon arbiter during the final age before the war.

Aurymathrax believed direct Dragon intervention would itself destroy balance and repeatedly refused decisive action.

Modern Dragon culture still carries immense shame tied to his failure.

---

## Vorthalyss — The Gilded Tyrant

An infamous Dragon whose obsession with wealth and material splendor became legendary even among Dragonkind.

Many historians consider Vorthalyss symbolic of Dragonkind's growing abandonment of their cosmic responsibilities before the fall of the worlds.

# Major Events of the Celestial War

The following events are among the most important surviving mythological and historical moments associated with the Celestial War.

Due to the immense age of the era, surviving accounts are often contradictory, symbolic, or heavily mythologized.

---

## The First Bestowal

### *The Immortalizing of the First Elves*

The First Bestowal marks the moment the Angelic mage Orelas successfully performed the Rite of Continuance upon the proto-elven peoples under his care.

For countless generations Orelas had watched mortal civilizations flourish briefly before fading into death and extinction. Unable to accept the endless loss of mortal life, he devoted himself to discovering a means of preserving creation beyond the limitations of the natural cycle.

The Rite of Continuance dramatically extended the lifespan of the proto-elves and slowed the decay of their souls.

Though celebrated by the Angels as a miraculous achievement, the rite unknowingly disrupted the ancient cycle of souls between Gontkhael and Nythorak.

For the first time in known existence, souls stopped returning properly between worlds.

Ancient Devil records describe the imbalance as:

> "The stilling of the wheel."

Many later Elven cultures mythologized the First Bestowal as the divine origin of Elven longevity itself.

---

## The Slaughter of Lethariel

### *The First Massacre*

The Slaughter of Lethariel is widely considered the true beginning of the Celestial War.

After repeated pleas to the Dragons failed, the Devil leadership ultimately sanctioned direct intervention to restore the disrupted balance between worlds.

The Devil General Maelthoryx led an assault against the immortalized proto-elven civilization hidden within the sacred valleys of Lethariel.

Ancient accounts describe entire silver forests burning beneath blackened skies while the immortalized Elves were systematically slaughtered.

Orelas was killed during the massacre while attempting to protect fleeing civilians.

The Angelic warrior Valacir arrived too late to prevent the destruction of the city, but held the Devil host long enough for survivors to escape deeper into the wilds.

During the battle Valacir slew Maelthoryx before ultimately falling himself.

Among Angels and later Demons, the Slaughter of Lethariel became synonymous with betrayal, martyrdom, and the death of peace between worlds.

Among Devils, the event remains one of the most controversial actions in their ancient history.

---

## The Sundering Debate

### *The Last Conclave of Balance*

The Sundering Debate was the final known diplomatic gathering between Angels, Devils, and Dragons before the conflict escalated into open apocalypse.

The conclave was held within the crystalline halls of Aurum Vael, a neutral Dragon sanctuary suspended above the convergence point between worlds.

Representatives from all three immortal races attended.

Aurelioneth argued that existence itself could evolve beyond the old cycle.

Vaelzira argued that the disruption already threatened the foundations of reality.

Aurymathrax pleaded for restraint and continued mediation.

No agreement was reached.

Modern Dragon traditions often identify this moment as the point at which Dragonkind irreversibly failed its purpose.

Many later myths claim the great hall itself cracked during the debate, an omen later interpreted as the first fracture of the coming Shattering.

---

## The Silence of Dragons

### *The Withdrawing of the Arbiters*

As the war escalated, Dragonkind became increasingly divided over whether direct intervention would preserve balance or destroy it entirely.

Some Dragons advocated immediate action.

Others argued neutrality was necessary.

Many had already become consumed by mortal affairs, territorial rivalries, and material obsession.

Unable to reach consensus, the Dragons largely withdrew from the conflict.

This period became known as the Silence of Dragons.

The phrase survived long after the war itself and is still used in the modern age to describe catastrophic inaction in the face of growing disaster.

Modern Dragons continue to carry deep inherited shame associated with this era.

---

## The Battle of Hollow Heaven

### *The Falling of the Seven Stars*

The Battle of Hollow Heaven marked one of the first moments the Celestial War visibly damaged reality itself.

The Angelic hosts under Seraphelle attempted to seize a major Devil stronghold along the boundary between worlds.

The resulting conflict lasted for what later myths describe as "seven burning days."

During the battle, seven immense celestial bodies fell from the heavens onto Gontkhael.

The impact shattered entire regions and permanently destabilized nearby magical currents.

Some modern scholars believe these falling stars were not stars at all, but fragments of broken planar gateways between worlds.

Ancient accounts claim the skies remained darkened for decades afterward.

The battle is often viewed as the moment both sides realized the war could destroy existence itself.

---

## The Burning of Gontkhael

### *The Dying of the First World*

As the war spread across Gontkhael and Nythorak, reality itself began collapsing beneath the strain.

Ancient records describe:

* Oceans evaporating into storms of crystal ash
* Entire cities vanishing overnight
* Regions consumed by celestial fire
* Gravity failing
* Time fracturing unpredictably
* Magic becoming unstable and violent

By this stage even many among the Angels and Devils no longer believed victory was possible.

The Burning of Gontkhael is remembered not as a single battle, but as the slow death of an entire world.

Many later Demon traditions describe this era as:

> "The age when the world screamed."

---

## The Discovery Beneath the World

### *The Awakening of the Soulstone*

While studying deep beneath the collapsing foundations of Gontkhael, the Angelic scholar Zalethira uncovered references to a vast primordial crystal embedded within the world's core.

This entity became known as the Soulstone.

Ancient texts describe the Soulstone as:

* the metaphysical heart of Gontkhael,
* the source of its life,
* and the anchor of its reality.

Though fascinated by the discovery, Zalethira repeatedly warned that no living being should ever attempt to wield its power.

After Zalethira's death during the war, her apprentice Amareth continued her work in secret as the world approached annihilation.

Eventually Amareth discovered how the Soulstone could be harnessed.

Some myths claim he wept upon realizing what the process required.

---

## The Final Invocation

### *The Shattering*

Facing total collapse, the Angels ultimately chose to wield the Soulstone as a final desperate act to end the war.

The exact details of the Final Invocation have been lost to time.

Most surviving traditions agree only on the outcome.

The Devils were defeated and cast into the Void.

The Soulstone shattered.

Gontkhael broke apart.

Reality itself fractured.

The surviving Angels were permanently transformed.

The event became known simply as:

### The Shattering

Among later Demon traditions, the immediate aftermath is often called:

### The Ashen Dawn

The moment the surviving Angels awoke to find their world destroyed, their immortality broken, and their own forms forever changed.

The Shattering marked the end of the Celestial Age and the beginning of all subsequent history.

# Legendary Figures and Events of the Mortal Age

*Working Canon*

---

## The First Stewards

Following the Shattering and the collapse of the ancient cycle, countless souls displaced from Gontkhael and Nythorak began arriving upon Eryndor.

The Dragons, burdened by guilt over their failure during the Celestial War, took responsibility for guiding these souls and cultivating life upon the surviving world.

Though Dragons remained the supreme stewards of Eryndor, they could not personally oversee every corner of the vast world.

To aid them, powerful guardian beings were created, awakened, or elevated across the continents.

These beings became known collectively as the First Stewards.

The First Stewards shaped the lands, guided emerging peoples, and helped cultivate the civilizations that would eventually become the mortal races of the modern age.

Many later cultures worshipped these beings as gods, though they were never truly divine.

Most eventually vanished, withdrew, or perished long before recorded history began.

Only fragments of their stories remain today.

---

### The Dragons of Eldressa

The Dragons served as the primary stewards of Eldressa and remained the closest thing to direct custodians of mortal civilization.

Unlike the other First Stewards, the Dragons did not merely shape the land itself. They actively guided the emerging races, taught agriculture and craftsmanship, mediated disputes, and helped establish the foundations of civilization.

Many of the earliest Elven kingdoms, Dwarven holds, and Human settlements trace portions of their mythology to ancient Dragon mentors and protectors.

The Age of Dragon Stewardship would eventually become one of the most celebrated and romanticized periods in Eldressan history.

---

### The Woodland Guardians of Ilyndor

The Woodland Guardians were immense primordial beings that presided over the Heartwood and the deepest forests of Ilyndor.

Neither beasts nor gods, they embodied the living spirit of the natural world itself.

Each Guardian was associated with a particular aspect of nature such as:

* forests,
* rivers,
* seasons,
* beasts,
* growth,
* decay,
* or renewal.

Though mortal, they possessed lifespans so vast that entire civilizations could rise and fall during a single Guardian's life.

The peoples of Ilyndor often revered them as deities, while the Deonati regard them as their ancestral progenitors and spiritual forebears.

Most Woodland Guardians vanished long ago, though stories persist that some still wander the deepest and most sacred regions of Ilyndor.

---

### The Forge Titans of Velkrand

The Forge Titans were colossal beings of living stone, crystal, magma, and metal.

They roamed the mountains, caverns, and volcanic regions of Velkrand during the earliest ages of the world.

The Forge Titans shaped the land itself through their movements.

Mountain ranges rose where they slept.
Ore veins formed in their footsteps.
Volcanoes awakened where they gathered.

Both Goblin and Gnomish traditions claim that many of their earliest crafts, tools, and techniques were inspired by observing the Forge Titans.

Goblin legends often portray them as masters of industry and endurance.

Gnomish traditions instead remember them as living embodiments of craftsmanship and creation.

The disappearance of the Forge Titans remains one of Velkrand's great mysteries.

Many ancient mechanisms and engineering marvels are still attributed to their influence.

---

### The Colossi of Hrothan

The Colossi were immense primordial beings composed of living stone, ice, and permafrost.

Among the oldest known creatures to inhabit Eryndor, many were so vast that early peoples mistook them for mountains, glaciers, or entire stretches of landscape.

Unlike the Dragons, the Colossi did not guide civilization.

Instead they embodied the harsh realities of Hrothan itself:

* endurance,
* patience,
* resilience,
* and survival.

Ancient migration routes, valleys, frozen rivers, and mountain passes are said to have been shaped by their movements.

The tribes of Hrothan viewed the Colossi with equal parts reverence and fear.

Many stories claim that entire generations survived by following the paths carved by wandering Colossi through otherwise impassable terrain.

Most are believed to have vanished long ago.

Some northern traditions insist they merely sleep beneath the ice, waiting for an age when they will once again cross the frozen world.

---

### The Leviathans of Skeldran

Before Skeldran became the shattered and storm-wracked wasteland known today, it was watched over by immense creatures known as the Leviathans.

These primordial beings were neither wholly terrestrial nor aquatic.

Some resembled colossal dragon turtles.
Others appeared as living islands, armored behemoths, or creatures whose forms defied easy description.

The Leviathans roamed both land and sea and were deeply tied to the continent's stability.

Ancient traditions claim that coastlines shifted in their wake and entire ecosystems flourished around their migrations.

Unlike the other First Stewards, the fate of the Leviathans is inseparable from the destruction of Skeldran itself.

Whatever catastrophe shattered the continent also brought an end to the age of the Leviathans.

Whether they perished, fled, transformed, or still slumber beneath the northern seas remains one of the oldest unsolved mysteries in the world.

Many sailors claim the fiercest storms of the northern oceans still carry echoes of their passing.

---

### The Meridian Isles

The Meridian Isles possessed no known First Steward.

For much of the First Age the islands remained wild, untamed, and largely uninhabited.

Treacherous waters and limited seafaring technology prevented large-scale settlement.

The islands existed largely beyond the influence of the great civilizations of the age and would not become important until much later in history.

## Eldressa

### Kaelor Icewalker

#### *The First Crossing*

A legendary human tribal leader believed to have led the first great human migration northward from Hrothan into Eldressa following the long age of wandering after the Shattering.

Ancient cairns, standing stones, and weathered road markers across the frozen south are often attributed to his people.

Most depictions portray Kaelor carrying a lantern said to contain dragonfire gifted by an ancient wyrm guide.

Whether Kaelor truly existed remains debated among historians.

---

### The Accord of Sky and Stone

One of the oldest surviving treaties in recorded myth.

The Accord was supposedly forged between the early Dwarven clans of the Claran Mountains and the Dragon steward Ithrymath.

The agreement established that the Dwarves would mine beneath the mountains while the Dragon guarded the peaks and deep passages from creatures emerging out of the underworld.

Many modern Dwarven holds still display broken fragments of tablets said to preserve portions of the Accord.

---

### The War of Ash and Iron

An ancient conflict between early Elven kingdoms and expanding Dwarven mountain holds.

The war began over control of the Embervein Range, whose mountains contained rare metals, magical crystal deposits, and ancient Dragon ruins.

Though neither side achieved decisive victory, the war permanently shaped relations between Elves and Dwarves for centuries afterward.

Several ruined fortresses from the conflict still remain abandoned in the mountains.

---

### The Battle of Hollow Pass

The most famous battle of the War of Ash and Iron.

A vastly outnumbered Dwarven force under Highthane Brokkir Stonevein held the narrow mountain pass of Kharum Vale against repeated Elven assaults for thirteen days.

According to legend, the defenders eventually collapsed the mountain itself rather than surrender the pass.

Modern Dwarven culture still treats the battle as a symbol of endurance and sacrifice.

---

### Elyra Windfoot

#### *The Lantern Queen*

A Halfling explorer, diplomat, and caravan leader credited with establishing many of the earliest trade routes across central Eldressa.

Stories claim Elyra traveled farther than any mortal of her age and carried a lantern said never to extinguish.

Many Halfling traditions regard her as the spiritual founder of the wandering caravan culture still common today.

Numerous roadside inns, waystones, and travelers' shrines bear her symbol.

---

### The Breach of Vel Anor

An ancient magical catastrophe caused when early Elven sorcerers attempted to recover relics believed to originate from shattered Gontkhael.

The resulting planar rupture consumed the city of Vel Anor and permanently scarred the surrounding forest.

The ruins remain abandoned.

Some claim whispers can still be heard there during certain nights.

---

### Thargrim Goldvein

#### *The King Beneath the Mountain*

One of the earliest great Dwarven rulers.

Thargrim unified dozens of feuding mountain clans and established the first true subterranean kingdom beneath the Claran Mountains.

Legends claim his throne was forged jointly by mortal smiths and Dragons.

His tomb has never been found.

Many treasure hunters believe it still exists somewhere beneath the deepest ruins of old Claran.

---

### The Silent Pilgrimage

A mysterious migration undertaken by several early Deonati clans after increasing conflict with expanding mortal civilizations.

The clans vanished westward into the ancient wilds long before the rise of modern Ilyndor.

Some believe this migration marked the true beginning of Deonati civilization within Ilyndor itself.

Very little reliable information survives regarding the journey.

---

### Seredain the White

#### *The Last Dragon Steward*

One of the final Dragons openly known to guide mortal civilization before Dragonkind largely withdrew from the affairs of the younger races.

Seredain served as mediator between early kingdoms for centuries and was widely revered across Eldressa.

Ancient statues depicting a great white Dragon remain standing in several ruined cities.

Many historians consider Seredain's disappearance symbolic of the end of the Age of Dragon Stewardship.

### The Crownless War

An ancient Elven civil conflict fought long before the rise of modern kingdoms.

The war began after the death of Queen Althiriel Vaelor, whose heirs each claimed the right to rule the growing Elven dominions of central Eldressa.

Unlike later mortal wars fought over territory or wealth, the Crownless War became deeply philosophical and ideological.

Different Elven houses argued over:

* the role of magic,
* the preservation of ancient traditions,
* relations with Dragons,
* and whether younger races should be guided or left to develop freely.

The conflict lasted generations and permanently fractured early Elven unity.

Many scholars believe the origins of several modern Elven cultures and political traditions can be traced back to the divisions created during this war.

Some ancient songs claim the forests themselves withered in grief during its bloodiest years.

---

### The Weeping of Brethil

A legendary tragedy remembered throughout Elven history.

During the later years of the Crownless War, several rival Elven houses fought within the sacred heartlands of Brethil despite ancient vows forbidding bloodshed there.

The battle reportedly lasted through an entire season beneath constant rainfall and blackened skies.

According to legend, the ancient trees of Brethil began bleeding silver sap during the fighting.

When the conflict finally ended, surviving Elven leaders swore the Oath of Falling Leaves — a sacred pact forbidding warfare within the deepest groves of Brethil ever again.

Many modern Elves still consider the forests of Brethil sacred because of this event.

Ancient white stone memorials said to mark the graves of the fallen can still supposedly be found deep within the woods.

---

## Velkrand

### Grakka Embervein

#### *Mother of the Forge-Clans*

The first great Goblin leader remembered in Velkrand's oral traditions.

Grakka united dozens of competing volcanic clans beneath a single banner and established the earliest Goblin city-fortresses within the Embercrown Mountains.

She is remembered less as a conqueror and more as a survivor.

Many legends claim she personally led her people through volcanic eruptions, cave collapses, and famine during the harsh early centuries of Goblin settlement.

Nearly every Goblin clan traces part of its lineage back to one of the Forge-Clans founded during her rule.

---

### Elowen Runebloom

#### *The First Artificer Queen*

The earliest known Gnomish ruler.

Elowen is credited with organizing the scattered southern settlements into the first true Gnomish League.

She encouraged cooperation between scholars, mages, builders, and craftsmen, creating the foundations of what would later become Velkrand's tradition of magical engineering.

Many Gnomish universities still display her symbol: a silver gear encircling a rune.

---

### The Hundred-Year Rivalry

The collective name given to countless small wars fought between Goblin and Gnomish settlements.

Unlike the great wars of Eldressa, the Hundred-Year Rivalry was not a single conflict.

Instead it consisted of:

* Border disputes
* Mine seizures
* Trade conflicts
* Tunnel wars
* Sabotage campaigns
* Competing claims over mineral-rich regions

The rivalry lasted generations and produced dozens of famous heroes and villains on both sides.

Modern Velkranders often view the period with a strange mixture of embarrassment and nostalgia.

---

### General Varkesh Ironjaw

#### *The Tunnel Wolf*

One of the most famous Goblin commanders of the Hundred-Year Rivalry.

Varkesh became legendary for leading lightning-fast assaults through hidden tunnel networks beneath contested mountain ranges.

Though feared by his enemies, many later accounts portray him as unusually honorable for the era.

Several stories claim he routinely evacuated civilians before launching attacks.

---

### Lady Miralyn Gearwright

#### *The Sapphire Strategist*

Perhaps the most celebrated military leader in Gnomish history.

Miralyn successfully defended the Sapphire Marches against repeated Goblin incursions despite commanding far smaller forces.

She became famous for using illusion magic, false fortifications, and engineered terrain to defeat numerically superior enemies.

Her tactical writings are still studied throughout modern Velkrand.

---

### The Ashfall Catastrophe

The greatest disaster in Velkrand's recorded history.

When the Great Mountain was destroyed at the end of the First Demon War, immense shockwaves spread across the world.

Northern Velkrand suffered devastating volcanic eruptions.

Entire Goblin cities vanished beneath ash and lava.

Southern coastlines were simultaneously struck by enormous tsunamis.

Several major Gnomish ports were completely destroyed.

Both civilizations lost much of their ruling leadership within a matter of days.

Many historians view the Ashfall Catastrophe as the true end of the old Velkrand.

---

### The Accord of Twin Hammers

The treaty that ended centuries of Goblin-Gnome conflict.

In the aftermath of the Ashfall Catastrophe, only two senior leaders remained alive.

#### Rukka Blackforge

*Last surviving High Matron of the Goblin Forge-Clans*

#### Cedric Runemark

*Last surviving Chancellor of the Gnomish League*

Together they met amid the ruins of the Great Mine and negotiated a permanent peace.

The treaty became known as the Accord of Twin Hammers.

Modern Velkrand still traces much of its legal and political tradition back to this agreement.

Many Goblins and Gnomes consider the two leaders among the greatest heroes in Velkrand's history.

---

### Torvin Cogwright & Nessa Sparkweaver

#### *The Makers of the First Clank*

A Goblin engineer and Gnomish artificer who worked together during the reconstruction era.

Seeking laborers capable of rebuilding the devastated continent, they created the earliest Clank prototypes.

These first creations were little more than sophisticated automatons.

Though useful, they possessed no true consciousness.

Most historians regard this partnership as the symbolic beginning of modern Velkrand.

---

### The Awakening of Unit Seven

The single most important event in Clank history.

According to tradition, a reconstruction automaton known only as Unit Seven unexpectedly refused a command issued by its creators.

When questioned, it reportedly replied:

> "Because I wished otherwise."

Whether the story is literal or symbolic remains debated.

Nevertheless, Clank culture universally recognizes Unit Seven as the first of their kind to demonstrate true self-awareness.

Modern Clank often celebrate the date of the Awakening as a cultural holiday.

---

### Thalan Rootspeaker

#### *Voice of the Greenwood*

The most revered Firbolg figure in recorded history.

While Goblins and Gnomes fought their endless rivalries, Thalan worked to preserve peace within the central forests.

He is credited with establishing many of the sacred woodland agreements that still protect portions of Velkrand's ancient forests.

Numerous Firbolg communities trace their traditions back to his teachings.

---

### The Gathering of Three Paths

A rare event in which Goblin, Gnome, and Firbolg leaders gathered peacefully during the reconstruction following the Ashfall Catastrophe.

The Gathering established the principle that Velkrand's future would be built not by conquest, but by cooperation.

Many modern historians consider this event the true birth of Velkrand as a shared civilization rather than merely a continent occupied by competing peoples.

## Ilyndor

### Aiyana Spiritwalker

#### *Mother of the First Deonati*

One of the oldest and most revered figures in Deonati tradition.

Though many details have been lost to myth, Aiyana is said to have been among the first Deonati to recognize the bond between the emerging mortal peoples of Ilyndor and the Woodland Guardians.

Stories describe her traveling throughout the continent learning from the various Guardians and carrying their teachings between distant tribes.

Many Deonati clans trace their spiritual traditions and ancestral customs back to her journeys.

To this day she is remembered not as a ruler, but as a teacher and unifier.

---

### The Great Gathering of Antlers

One of the earliest continent-wide events remembered in Ilyndoran history.

Representatives of dozens of Deonati, Katari, Faerie, Faun, and Galapa communities gathered beneath the ancient boughs of the Heartwood to establish peaceful coexistence between the emerging peoples of Ilyndor.

Unlike the treaties of Eldressa, the Gathering was never written down.

Instead, each people carried part of the agreement through songs, stories, dances, carvings, and oral tradition.

Many modern Ilyndoran customs trace their origins to this event.

---

### The Path of Falling Leaves

A legendary migration undertaken by several Deonati clans during the early First Age.

Led by the explorer and spiritual guide Naru Windtail, these clans crossed the western seas and established the first lasting Deonati settlements within Eldressa.

The reasons for the migration remain debated.

Some traditions claim the Woodland Guardians foresaw coming changes in the balance of the world.

Others claim the journey was undertaken simply because the Deonati believed new experiences strengthened the soul.

Whatever the cause, the migration permanently connected the histories of Ilyndor and Eldressa.

Many of the Deonati peoples found within Eldressa today trace their ancestry back to the Path of Falling Leaves.

---

### Naru Windtail

#### *The Pathfinder*

The Deonati leader most often associated with the Path of Falling Leaves.

Stories portray Naru as endlessly curious and possessed of an almost supernatural sense of direction.

It is said that when asked how he found his way across unknown seas, he simply replied:

> "The world wished to be known."

Many Deonati explorers still invoke his name before beginning long journeys.

---

### Lethriel Moonwing

#### *The First Fairy Queen*

The oldest named figure preserved within Faerie tradition.

Lethriel is remembered as a mediator between the Faerie courts and the mortal peoples of Ilyndor during the earliest days of civilization.

Legends claim she possessed wings woven from moonlight itself and could travel freely between the waking world and the realm of dreams.

Many stories portray her resolving conflicts not through force but through riddles, bargains, and impossible challenges.

Though often called a queen, she is remembered more as a judge and keeper of balance than a monarch.

---

### The Covenant of Moon and Root

An ancient agreement said to have been forged between the Faerie Courts and the Woodland Guardians.

The Covenant established sacred boundaries protecting portions of the Heartwood from exploitation and ensured that neither the Fair Folk nor the mortal races would claim dominion over the deepest forests.

Many of Ilyndor's oldest sacred groves are said to remain protected by the terms of this covenant.

Whether the agreement still holds is a matter of considerable debate.

---

### Kael Thornwarden

#### *The First Guardian*

The most revered figure in Katari history.

Kael is remembered as one of the first defenders of the Heartwood and the founder of many traditions still practiced by Katari guardians today.

According to legend, Kael spent decades walking the boundaries of the Heartwood, learning every path, stream, and hidden glade.

When conflicts arose between expanding settlements and sacred lands, Kael often served as mediator, protector, and judge.

Modern Katari commonly trace their duty to safeguard the Heartwood directly back to his teachings.

---

### The Silent Bloom

A mysterious event remembered throughout Ilyndor.

During a single spring season, every sacred tree within the Heartwood reportedly flowered simultaneously.

Woodland Guardians emerged openly from the deep forests.

Ancient rivalries ceased.

Predators hunted beside prey.

Even the Faerie Courts suspended their disputes.

The reason for the event has never been explained.

Many spiritual traditions view the Silent Bloom as one of the rare moments when all life within Ilyndor existed in perfect harmony.

To this day the phrase:

> "May you see the Silent Bloom"

remains a blessing throughout much of the continent.

### The Withering of House Silverbloom

One of the oldest tragedies preserved within Faerie folklore.

House Silverbloom was once among the most influential of the early Faerie Courts.

Their matriarch, Lady Sylthara Silverbloom, sought to preserve beauty forever and became obsessed with preventing change, decay, and death.

Over many years she used increasingly powerful enchantments to halt the passage of time within her domain.

At first her lands flourished.

Flowers never wilted.

Fruit never spoiled.

No creature aged.

Eventually nothing changed at all.

The forests fell silent.

Animals ceased reproducing.

Even the winds stopped blowing.

When the Woodland Guardians finally intervened, the entire court vanished.

To this day travelers speak of a hidden valley where flowers bloom eternally and nothing has moved for thousands of years.

---

### The Feast of Hollow Antlers

A dark chapter in early Deonati history.

During a period of famine, several isolated clans abandoned ancient customs and turned to practices now considered taboo.

Led by a charismatic shaman known only as Hollow Antler, these clans began hunting sacred beasts associated with the Woodland Guardians.

The resulting prosperity was short-lived.

Stories claim strange afflictions spread through the clans.

Children were born without animal traits.

Dreams became plagued by unnatural whispers.

Eventually the clans disappeared entirely.

Among many Deonati cultures, the phrase:

> "Do not follow the Hollow Antler."

remains a warning against abandoning tradition for short-term gain.

---

### The Blackroot Blight

One of the greatest ecological disasters in Ilyndoran memory.

A mysterious corruption spread through portions of the Heartwood, turning ancient forests black and causing plants to grow into twisted and hostile forms.

Entire communities vanished.

Several Woodland Guardians reportedly perished attempting to contain the spread.

The source of the blight was never discovered.

Though eventually contained, small pockets of Blackroot still occasionally appear throughout Ilyndor.

Many believe the corruption never truly died.

It merely sleeps.

---

### Sereth the Thorn King

Perhaps the most feared figure in Ilyndoran folklore.

Sereth was once a respected Katari guardian entrusted with protecting a sacred region of the Heartwood.

Over centuries he became convinced that mortals themselves were the greatest threat to nature.

His philosophy grew increasingly extreme.

Eventually he declared that all civilized peoples should be removed from the forests entirely.

Several settlements were destroyed before other Katari and Woodland Guardians united to stop him.

The exact circumstances of his defeat remain unclear.

Many stories claim he was transformed into an enormous thorn-covered tree somewhere deep within the Heartwood.

Others insist he still wanders the deepest forests searching for intruders.

---

### The Night of Falling Stars

A mysterious event remembered across nearly every culture of Ilyndor.

For a single night, thousands of lights reportedly fell from the heavens into the western jungles.

The following morning entire regions had changed.

New rivers appeared.

Ancient ruins emerged from beneath the earth.

Several previously unknown species of plants and animals were discovered.

No explanation has ever been found.

Many scholars believe the event may have involved one of the fragmented realities created by the Shattering.

The Faerie Courts refuse to discuss the matter.

Which only fuels speculation further.

---

### The Silent Court

The most mysterious of all known Faerie Courts.

Unlike the better-known Courts of Moonlight and Bloom, the Silent Court rarely interacted with mortals.

Ancient records suggest they were responsible for guarding something hidden beneath the Heartwood.

One day the Court simply disappeared.

Their palaces were found abandoned.

Their banners untouched.

Their guardians gone.

No signs of struggle were ever discovered.

Modern Faeries generally avoid speaking of the Silent Court.

When asked, many simply reply:

> "Some doors remain closed for a reason."

### Sayuri Nine-Trails

#### *The Laughing Pathfinder*

Perhaps the most beloved figure in all Deonati folklore.

Sayuri was a Kitsune wanderer, storyteller, diplomat, and explorer who traveled throughout Ilyndor during the First Age.

Unlike many legendary figures remembered for great battles or magical feats, Sayuri became famous simply for bringing distant peoples together.

Stories claim she visited nearly every major settlement in Ilyndor and was welcomed by Deonati clans, Faerie Courts, Katari guardians, Woodland Guardians, and even creatures normally hostile to mortals.

She is credited with carrying songs, stories, customs, and knowledge between isolated communities, helping shape a shared Ilyndoran identity long before any formal political structures existed.

Many tales describe her outwitting powerful spirits, resolving disputes through humor, and leaving behind gifts or advice that later changed lives.

A common saying throughout Ilyndor remains:

> "Walk lightly enough and even Sayuri may leave you a trail."

---

### The Festival of Returning Wings

One of the oldest celebrations still observed across portions of Ilyndor.

According to tradition, a great flock of luminous birds descended upon the Heartwood during the First Age after a devastating winter.

The birds remained for an entire season, filling the forests with brilliant colors and song.

Their arrival became associated with renewal, hope, and the return of life after hardship.

Though the birds themselves were never seen again, annual festivals commemorating the event spread across much of the continent.

Today the Festival of Returning Wings is celebrated through lantern releases, music, storytelling, and communal feasts.

Many Ilyndoran peoples consider it a reminder that difficult seasons never last forever.

---

### The Song of Seven Rivers

A legendary journey undertaken by seven musicians from seven different peoples of Ilyndor.

The travelers sought to create a single song capable of representing the entire continent.

Over many years they traveled together through jungles, forests, mountains, coastlines, and sacred groves, learning the traditions of countless communities.

When they finally returned to the Heartwood, they performed their composition before the Woodland Guardians themselves.

Legend claims every creature within earshot fell silent to listen.

The original song has long since been lost.

However, many musical traditions across Ilyndor claim descent from fragments of the Seven Rivers melody.

Among bards and storytellers, the phrase:

> "Adding your verse to the Seven Rivers"

remains synonymous with contributing something meaningful to the world.

## Hrothan

### The Long Winter

The defining catastrophe of early Hrothan.

For generations the peoples of the north relied heavily upon enormous migratory tundra beasts known as Icehorns.

The creatures provided:

* food,
* leather,
* bone,
* shelter materials,
* and fuel.

Then, almost without warning, the herds began to die.

Different traditions offer different explanations.

Some blame disease.

Others claim the Colossi themselves altered the migration routes.

Still others insist a curse fell upon the land.

Whatever the cause, the collapse of the Icehorn herds brought famine throughout Hrothan.

Human tribes began competing over increasingly scarce resources.

Conflicts with Goliath clans and Giant territories became increasingly common.

Many historians consider the Long Winter the first event that ultimately led to the Human Migration into Eldressa.

---

### Kaelor Icewalker

#### *The First Pathfinder*

The most famous figure in Human history.

Kaelor is believed to have led the first great migration southward from Hrothan into Eldressa.

Stories portray him less as a warrior and more as a guide.

He is said to have spent years mapping routes through glaciers, mountain passes, and frozen coastlines before leading his people south.

Most depictions show him carrying a lantern said to contain Dragonfire gifted by an ancient wyrm.

Whether Kaelor truly existed remains debated.

Nevertheless, nearly every Human culture preserves some version of his story.

---

### Lyra Snowsong

#### *The Last Voice of the North*

One of the most beloved figures in Human folklore.

Lyra lived during the height of the Long Winter and is credited with preserving many of the oral histories that survived the migration.

She traveled between scattered tribes gathering songs, stories, and genealogies before entire communities disappeared.

Many modern Human epics are said to descend from the Snowsongs she composed.

Among northern peoples she remains a symbol of memory and perseverance.

---

### The Battle of Broken Tusks

One of the largest conflicts fought during the Long Winter.

Several starving Human tribes attempted to follow the last major Icehorn migration into territory traditionally controlled by the Stoneback Goliaths.

The resulting conflict lasted several weeks and claimed hundreds of lives.

Though neither side achieved decisive victory, the battle convinced many Human leaders that Hrothan could no longer support all its peoples indefinitely.

Many historians view it as a major catalyst for the later migration south.

---

### Hrogar Stoneborn

#### *The First High Chieftain*

The most famous Goliath leader of the First Age.

Hrogar united numerous mountain clans during the chaos of the Long Winter and successfully defended the northern highlands against both Giants and Human incursions.

Though often remembered as a warrior, many traditions emphasize his role as a lawgiver and mediator.

Several ancient Goliath customs are attributed to his leadership.

---

### The Oath of Three Peaks

A rare peace agreement between Humans, Goliaths, and Giants.

Negotiated during one of the harshest periods of famine, the Oath established temporary hunting boundaries and migration rights between dozens of competing groups.

Though the agreement eventually collapsed, it prevented widespread bloodshed for nearly a generation.

Many later diplomatic traditions in Hrothan trace their origins to this event.

---

### Skara White-Eye

#### *The Giant Seer*

One of the oldest named Giants remembered in Hrothan.

Unlike many Giant leaders, Skara was known not for strength but for wisdom.

She reportedly foresaw the coming collapse of the Icehorn herds decades before it occurred.

Many Giant oral traditions claim she urged cooperation between the peoples of Hrothan, though few listened.

Some stories suggest she predicted the Human Migration long before it began.

---

### The Crossing of Frostfire Strait

The event that formally began the Human Migration.

Led by Kaelor Icewalker and several allied tribal leaders, thousands of Humans crossed the frozen strait separating Hrothan from the southern lands.

Many perished during the journey.

Those who survived would eventually become the ancestors of most Human peoples living in Eldressa today.

Among Human cultures, the Crossing is often regarded as the moment humanity chose hope over survival.

---

### Jorund Bearcloak

#### *The Wanderer of the White World*

A legendary explorer whose journeys became famous throughout Hrothan.

Jorund traveled farther north than any known mortal and returned with stories of:

* sleeping Colossi,
* moving glaciers,
* impossible lights,
* and frozen seas that stretched beyond the horizon.

Many believe his stories were wildly exaggerated.

Others insist he discovered truths about Hrothan that were never meant to be known.

His journals have never been found.

---

### The Last Hunt

A legendary event said to mark the end of the old Hrothan.

According to tradition, the final great Icehorn herd was tracked by representatives of Humans, Goliaths, and Giants alike.

Rather than fight over the remaining animals, the leaders chose to hunt together.

The feast that followed became one of the few moments of unity remembered by all three peoples.

Many storytellers regard the Last Hunt as the symbolic end of the Age of Wandering and the beginning of a new chapter in Hrothan's history.

### The Gathering of a Thousand Fires

The largest peaceful assembly in recorded Hrothan history.

Following decades of famine and conflict, representatives of Human tribes, Goliath clans, and several Giant lineages gathered upon the shores of Lake Varglas.

For seven nights thousands of fires burned across the frozen lake.

Old feuds were settled.

Marriages were arranged.

Trade routes were established.

Songs and stories were exchanged.

Many historians regard the Gathering as one of the rare moments when the peoples of Hrothan viewed themselves not as rivals, but as fellow survivors of the same harsh world.

### Lake Varglas
#### *The Frozen Mirror*

The largest freshwater lake in Hrothan and one of the most revered locations in the north.

For most of the year Varglas appears as an endless expanse of ice stretching beyond the horizon.

Many northern traditions claim the lake was formed when a dying Colossus lay down upon the tundra and slowly sank into the earth, leaving behind a basin so immense that it filled with meltwater.

The lake serves as both a trade route and a sacred place.

Entire clans travel across its frozen surface during winter migrations.

According to legend, on particularly clear nights the ice becomes transparent enough to reveal visions of the past beneath its surface.

Whether these visions are real or merely stories remains a matter of debate.

### The Sleeping Beneath

A mystery shared by many cultures of Hrothan.

Numerous stories claim that something enormous lies dormant beneath Lake Varglas.

Descriptions vary dramatically.

Some traditions speak of an ancient Colossus.

Others claim it is a forgotten Leviathan that somehow reached the frozen north.

Still others insist it is merely a story invented to frighten children.

Every few generations strange tremors reportedly ripple across the lake without explanation.

The phenomenon has never been conclusively explained.

Most northerners agree on only one thing:

Whatever lies beneath Varglas should be left undisturbed.

### Eira Icebound
#### *The Keeper of Reflections*

One of the most celebrated storytellers in Hrothan's history.

Eira spent much of her life traveling the shores of Lake Varglas collecting stories from distant tribes.

She believed every story carried a fragment of truth and devoted herself to preserving them before they could be forgotten.

Many of the legends surrounding the Colossi, the Giants, and the Human Migration survive today because of her work.

Northern bards often claim that every storyteller learns from Eira, whether they know it or not.

The final gathering of the remaining Leviathans.

The war continued.

The final phase of the Leviathan War.

The surviving Leviathans unleashed devastation on a scale never before witnessed.

Entire regions sank.

Mountain ranges collapsed.

The seas swallowed great portions of the continent.

The once-unified landmass fractured into the shattered Skeldran known today.

By the war's end, all nine Leviathans had perished.

### The Silence of the Leviathans

The day the final Leviathan died.

Many traditions regard this as the end of the First Age of Skeldran.

The surviving peoples scattered.

Their civilizations collapsed.

The age of Leviathan stewardship came to an end forever.

# The Modern Age

**Status:** Working Canon

The Modern Age began with the end of the Second Demon War.

Following the sealing of the Void Rift and the establishment of peace between the peoples of Eryndor and the Demons of Gontkhael, historians generally mark this event as **The Unification**.

Though the world remained politically divided, the Unification ended an age of existential conflict and ushered in a new era of diplomacy, trade, exploration, and cooperation between the peoples of the known world.

Most modern calendars measure time relative to this event.

Dates are commonly recorded as:

* **BU** — Before Unification
* **AU** — After Unification

Examples:

* 500 BU — Five hundred years before the Unification
* 125 AU — One hundred and twenty-five years after the Unification
* 312 AU — Three hundred and twelve years after the Unification

The current year is approximately **300 AU**.

---

## The Three Centuries Since Unification

The roughly three centuries following the Unification are collectively known as the Modern Age.

This period witnessed:

* The rebuilding of lands devastated by the Second Demon War.
* The gradual return of Dragon influence throughout the world.
* The growth of maritime trade and exploration.
* The rise of Meridia as the diplomatic center of the known world.
* Increasing cooperation between the major continents.
* The emergence of many of the modern nations, alliances, and institutions that define the current age.

Though the great wars of previous eras have ended, the world remains far from peaceful. Ancient rivalries, political ambitions, economic competition, and forgotten dangers continue to shape the course of history.

The events of the Modern Age remain recent enough to be well documented, yet distant enough that the Second Demon War itself has passed from living memory for most races.

## The Reconstruction Era (0–80 AU)

The first decades following the Unification were dedicated almost entirely to rebuilding.

The Second Demon War had devastated much of Eldressa, particularly the southern regions surrounding Rivara and the former frontiers of the war.

At the same time, many Demons chose to remain upon Eryndor following the peace accords rather than return to the dying remnants of Gontkhael.

Though tensions remained, the shared hardships of the war led to cooperation between many mortal peoples and the newly arrived Demon communities.

Cities were rebuilt.

Roads were restored.

Trade slowly resumed.

Throughout Eldressa, old kingdoms struggled to recover from centuries of warfare.

Meanwhile, Velkrand underwent a transformation of its own.

The Goblin city-states, Gnomish leagues, Firbolg enclaves, and growing Clank communities increasingly found cooperation more profitable than rivalry. What began as economic partnerships gradually evolved into broader political agreements, eventually leading to what historians now refer to as the Velkrand Consolidation.

By the end of the Reconstruction Era, Velkrand had emerged as one of the most stable and prosperous regions in the known world.

Though much of the world remained isolated and focused on local recovery, the foundations of the modern age had been laid.

---

## The Age of Reconnection (80–170 AU)

The event that transformed the world occurred near the end of the Reconstruction Era.

After centuries of absence, the Dragons began to return.

The reasons for their disappearance and eventual return remain the subject of considerable debate among scholars.

Unlike the Dragon Stewards of ancient history, the returning Dragons did not seek to govern the mortal races.

Instead, they acted as ambassadors, explorers, teachers, and protectors.

For the first time in recorded history, Dragons regularly traveled between continents.

Dragon riders emerged as messengers capable of crossing oceans and mountain ranges in a matter of days.

Information that once took months or years to travel could now spread across the world with unprecedented speed.

The peoples of Eldressa, Velkrand, Ilyndor, Hrothan, and even the isolated settlements of Skeldran found themselves increasingly connected.

Historians generally regard this period as the beginning of true globalization within Eryndor.

The return of the Dragons also transformed Eldressan politics.

For centuries the nations of Eldressa had operated largely independently, cooperating only when necessity demanded it. While the Second Demon War had forged temporary alliances, many old rivalries remained beneath the surface.

Seeking to prevent future conflicts and encourage cooperation, the Dragons helped broker the creation of Valtiron.

Constructed within the ancient crater left by the destruction of the Great Mountain during the First Demon War, Valtiron was envisioned as neutral ground belonging to no single kingdom.

Representatives from across Eldressa gathered there to negotiate treaties, resolve disputes, coordinate trade, and discuss matters affecting the continent as a whole.

Over time Valtiron grew into the political heart of Eldressa.

Though it governs no territory of its own, decisions made within its halls often shape the future of the entire continent.

Many historians regard the founding of Valtiron as one of the defining events of the Age of Reconnection.

---

## The Age of Discovery (170–250 AU)

As trade routes expanded and contact between continents increased, previously isolated regions began to play larger roles in world affairs.

The greatest beneficiary of this transformation was the Meridian Isles.

For most of history the islands had remained sparsely inhabited and largely ignored due to their remote location and difficult seas.

Now positioned at the crossroads of expanding maritime trade routes, the islands became a natural meeting place between continents.

Settlements multiplied rapidly.

Merchants, explorers, adventurers, scholars, and diplomats arrived from every corner of the world.

The city of Meridia grew from a modest port into one of the largest and most influential cities in the known world.

The Age of Discovery also witnessed:

* Increased exploration of Skeldran.
* Renewed contact between distant cultures.
* The spread of magical and technological innovations.
* Expansion of international trade networks.
* The establishment of many modern institutions and guilds.

---

## The Modern Era (250 AU – Present)

The current age is characterized by relative stability and unprecedented global interconnectedness.

Trade flourishes between continents.

Diplomatic relations are stronger than at any point in recorded history.

Dragon riders, merchant fleets, and international organizations regularly connect peoples who would once have lived their entire lives without ever hearing of one another.

While no conflict approaching the scale of the Demon Wars has occurred since the Unification, the era has not been entirely peaceful.

Various local conflicts, succession disputes, border wars, pirate kingdoms, and would-be conquerors have emerged over the centuries.

None, however, have proven capable of threatening the broader stability of the world.

The current year is approximately 300 AU.

Most people living today consider themselves part of a world far larger than the one their ancestors knew.

Yet beneath the prosperity of the modern age, ancient mysteries, old rivalries, and forgotten dangers remain.


# Modern Political Landscape

No single nation or continent dominates Eryndor.

Instead, power is distributed among regional governments, merchant coalitions, tribal confederations, diplomatic institutions, and influential individuals.

The centuries following the Unification have been remarkably stable by historical standards. While local conflicts, succession disputes, piracy, and territorial disagreements still occur, no conflict has approached the scale of the Demon Wars.

Three major centers of influence dominate modern world affairs:

* **Eldressa** — Political and cultural influence.
* **Velkrand** — Industrial and technological influence.
* **Meridia** — Diplomatic and economic influence.

Together they form a delicate balance that has maintained relative peace throughout the Modern Age.

## Modern Economic Landscape

The modern economy of Eryndor is more interconnected than at any previous point in history.

Founded in Rivara during the Reconstruction Era and later relocated to Meridia as global trade expanded, the Bank of Unification serves as the closest institution Eryndor possesses to a central bank.

Its primary mint remains in Velkrand, whose industrial infrastructure allows for the production of coinage on a scale unmatched elsewhere in the world.

Today the Bank maintains major branches in:

* Meridia
* Rivara
* Valtiron
* Steamreach
* Highcrag Spires
* Several Free Cities of Velkrand
* The resort isles of southern Ilyndor

## Major Trade Houses

### House Valemont

#### Rivara

One of the oldest surviving merchant dynasties in Eldressa.

House Valemont traces its history back to the centuries before the Second Demon War and survived largely through its extensive maritime network and political neutrality.

The family remains influential throughout Eldressa and controls significant portions of the luxury goods and agricultural export markets.

Many still regard House Valemont as the traditional face of Eldressan commerce.

**Current Leadership**

**Lord Aelric Valemont (High Elf, Male)**
Patriarch of House Valemont. A shrewd and patient merchant prince whose family has guided the house for generations. Though often viewed as conservative, few can match his political instincts.

**Seraphine Valemont (Human, Female)**
Granddaughter of Aelric and heir apparent to the house. Much more willing to embrace modern trade and foreign partnerships than her grandfather.

**Master Quartermaster Dorn Hillbarrow (Halfling, Male)**
Oversees much of the house's shipping and logistics network. Widely regarded as one of the most capable merchants in Rivara.

### The Argent Exchange

#### Valtiron

A comparatively young but rapidly growing mercantile consortium founded after the Unification.

Unlike the aristocratic House Valemont, the Argent Exchange is composed of investors, merchants, scholars, and financiers from across Eldressa.

The organization played a significant role in financing the construction and expansion of Valtiron.

Today it acts as one of the continent's most influential economic institutions and frequently competes with House Valemont for trade contracts and political influence.

**Current Leadership**

**Cassian Thornfield (Human, Male)**
Current Speaker of the Exchange. Born the son of a minor merchant family, Cassian represents the rise of a new merchant class following the Unification.

**Mira Goodbarrel (Halfling, Female)**
Director of Investments and one of the wealthiest individuals in Eldressa. Known for funding ambitious ventures others consider impossible.

**Zarinesha (Demon, Female)**
Director of International Trade. One of the most visible Demon business leaders in the modern world and a major advocate for continued cooperation between Demons and mortals.
